Roles say who can do what in a circle, the way a Discord server's do: make roles, put them in order, give them to people, and let each space change the rules for itself. Find them in Circle settings › Roles.

Owner, admins, roles and @everyone

WhoCan
OwnerEverything: give any role, make admins, and delete the circle. The person who made it.
AdminsEverything but deleting the circle or making admins. Made by the owner.
RolesWhat each role allows, in order: the higher a role is, the more it outranks. Nobody can manage a role at or above their own highest.
@everyoneWhat everyone in the circle may do, whatever their roles. Always under every role.

The seven permissions

PermissionLets people
Change the circleChange its name, picture, colour and icon, and turn plugins on for everyone.
Manage spacesAdd, rename, change, arrange and delete spaces.
Manage rolesMake and change roles, and give them to people.
Invite peopleBring people in, and see the invite link.
WriteSend messages in chat spaces.
TalkJoin voice spaces and talk.
Share filesAdd files in Files spaces.

Make a role

In Circle settings › Roles, tap New role. It's made at the bottom, over @everyone.

New role

Under Display, rename it and pick a colour. Names in chats are drawn in the colour of each person's highest role that has one.

Under Permissions, switch on what the role lets people do.

Under Members, tick who has it. Or tap someone in the circle's members list: their card has their roles to change.

Drag a role by its handle to move it up or down, or use Move up and Move down in its menu.

A space's own permissions

Each space can change the rules for itself: open the space's settings, then Permissions. For each role, each permission has three settings.

Here, an Announcements space where only moderators can write: Deny Write for @everyone, Allow it for Moderators. As the circle says leaves it to the circle's roles.

Removing someone

The owner or an admin can remove someone: tap them in the members list, then Remove. Their app leaves the circle, and nobody in it hears them again.

Removing someone stops their app joining the circle. But a circle's messages are sealed with a key everyone in it has, and that key doesn't change when someone leaves, so an old or altered app that kept the key could still read what's said. For anything they mustn't read, make a new circle and invite the people who should be in it.
