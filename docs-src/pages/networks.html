Link your other chat accounts and their conversations show in Messages beside your texts: read, reply, react, all in one place. Each is in Settings › Connections, one section per network. Your messages go between your device and that network directly, never through Uplink's server.

Official networks and plugins

Telegram and Signal are Uplink's official networks: they're there from the start, under Settings › Connections, ready to sign in to. (Your texts, through Google Messages, are separate and on by default.)

The others — WhatsApp, Matrix, Microsoft Teams, Google Chat, Messenger, Instagram, Discord, X and Slack — are plugins. They're off until you switch them on, and while off they don't run or show any chats. Open one under Connections › Plugins and turn on Use network to connect it; turn it off and it's signed out and left alone. Plugins aren't official Uplink connections: each signs in the way that network's own website or app does, and some don't allow other apps at all, so you agree to a warning first. If you'd already connected one, it stays on when you update.

Telegram

Open Settings › Connections › Telegram › Sign in to Telegram and type the number of your Telegram account, with its country code.

Next

Telegram sends a code to your Telegram app (or texts it). Type it in.

If your Telegram account has a cloud password, Uplink asks for it. Then your chats arrive.

On a computer, you can tap Sign in with your phone instead and approve it on your phone.

Signal

Open Settings › Connections › Signal › Link Signal. A QR code shows.

Scan this with Signal

On the phone where Signal is set up: Signal's Settings › Linked devices › Link new device, then scan the code. Uplink becomes one of your linked devices, like Signal's desktop app.

Messages from before you linked don't come over; everything from then on does.

WhatsApp

WhatsApp has no official way for other apps to link, and it may ban accounts it sees using unofficial apps. Uplink tells you this before linking. Only link an account you could live without.

WhatsApp is a plugin: open Settings › Connections › WhatsApp and turn on Use WhatsApp first. It shows the warning; tap I understand, link WhatsApp to go on.

Scan the QR code with WhatsApp's Linked devices › Link a device, or tap Link with phone number instead and type the 8-character code into WhatsApp.

Scan this with WhatsAppLink with phone number instead

Matrix

Any Matrix server works. Open Settings › Connections › Matrix, type your server or whole user id (like @you:matrix.org; empty means matrix.org), and sign in in the browser. Servers that still take a password have Or with a password. To read older encrypted messages, use Unlock older messages with your recovery key.

Unlinking

Open the network in Settings › Connections and sign out or unlink. Its chats leave Uplink; nothing is deleted from the network itself.

Discord, X and Slack

These three sign in with your browser's session, as Messenger and Instagram do: the browser extension does it for every device, or paste a request copied from the site's developer tools. Discord brings your direct messages and group chats (this is apart from linking a server's voice channels, which uses Uplink's bot). X brings your direct messages; they're end-to-end encrypted, so Uplink asks once for your 4-digit X Chat passcode (the one x.com/messages asks for) and doesn't keep it. Slack brings one workspace's channels and direct messages.

None of them allows other apps. Discord's terms forbid automating a user account and Discord can disable an account that does; X may lock or limit an account; a Slack workspace's admins may sign the session out.
