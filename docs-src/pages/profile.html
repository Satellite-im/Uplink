Your profile is what friends, and people in your spaces and circles, see of you. It lives in Settings › Me.

Name, picture and status

  • Picture and status (up to 80 characters) go to your friends only, never to the relay server. Your status shows in spaces and circles too.
  • Username is how people add you as a friend. Give up @… lets anyone take it after you; your friends stay your friends.
  • Show friends my number and linked accounts puts them on the card friends see when they tap your face or @name.

Anyone can tap your face or @name in a space to see your card: picture, status, and buttons to add you or write to you.

Verify your phone number

Verifying your number lets friends who have it find you, and lets members of a circle with an SMS space get its messages by text without anyone adding them. It's optional.

Settings › Me › Number says Not verified. Tap it.

Check the number and tap Text me a code.

Text me a code

On your phone, the code is read from the text as it arrives; you can type it too. It lasts ten minutes. On a computer, it's verified through your paired phone.

Texted to +1 555 010 0199.

Your number is never shown to anyone unless you switch on Show friends my number and linked accounts. Remove it any time: tap Number, then Remove verification.

Linked accounts and friends joining

Under Linked accounts, link your Discord, Telegram or X account by signing in to it. Then friends who look for that handle can find you. Bluesky, Mastodon and GitHub are .

Settings › Friends joining works the other way: add the handles of people you know on Discord, Telegram or X, and you're told when they join Uplink. Those apps don't let another app read your friend list, so you add them yourself.

Remove everything about me unlinks every account and your number, and clears your watch list, on the phone and on the server.

Your account key

Your account is known on the network by a key, the same on every device you use. Settings › Me › Profile shows it written as a did:key, a standard way to write a key as text, with Copy and Share. A friend's card shows theirs.

  • Sign a statement: write up to 280 characters, like "I am @alex on Uplink", and you get a block of text to paste in a bio, a post or a page elsewhere. It shows those words came from your account, and when.
  • Check a statement: paste a block to see whose it is: yours, a friend's, or an account you don't know. Nothing is sent anywhere to check it.
  • Anyone can check a statement without the app or our servers: the block names the account's key, and the signature is a standard one (P-256 with SHA-256). The format, and a small script that checks one, are in the app's source.

Vouching for friends

On a friend's card, Vouch for @name says their account really is them, for a year. The card says when yours runs out; Vouch for @name again renews it for another year, and you can take it back whenever you like.

@maya · Vouched for by 2 of your friends Vouch for @maya
  • A card counts only the vouches of your own friends, and tapping the count shows their names. There's no count of everyone. Your vouch reaches your friend's friends with their profile, but nobody else, and each of them counts it only if you are their friend too.
  • On a computer paired with your phone, vouching is done on the phone.
  • Your vouch goes to that friend, sealed, and travels with their profile to their friends. Each one is signed by your key, so nobody can make one up in your name, the relay included.
  • In Profile › Vouches you see who vouches for you, and can hide them from your friends. Removing or blocking a friend drops their vouch.
