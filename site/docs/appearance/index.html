Looks, colours and fonts

The app's looks, making and sharing your own, and the Readable font.

How the app looks: one of its looks or one you make, light or dark, the colour of your bubbles, the size of the words, and a font made for reading. All in Settings › Appearance.

Language

The app speaks twenty-eight languages: English, Arabic, Bengali, Chinese (Simplified), Dutch, French, German, Greek, Hebrew, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Marathi, Persian, Polish, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Swahili, Tamil, Telugu, Thai, Turkish, Ukrainian, Urdu and Vietnamese. In Arabic, Hebrew, Persian and Urdu the whole app is mirrored, right to left; a message written in another language keeps its own direction. Settings › Language: Automatic follows your device's language where the app has it, and English otherwise; or choose one. It changes at once, on every page.

Most of the app is translated. Words the app puts together as it goes (a name, a count, a date) and anything not yet translated stay in English. Only what you see changes: messages, names and everything sent to other people are left as they were written.

Looks

The app comes with four looks: Uplink (its own), Sharp, Dial-Up and Runestone. Tap one to use it. Each brings its own colour for your bubbles, which you can still change under Bubbles. Uplink and Sharp have a light and a dark half and follow Theme: System, Light or Dark. Dial-Up is always light, and Runestone always dark.

Textures

Under the looks, Textures has the packs of the look you are using: what it is drawn with. The first row is the look as it comes, with no pack. Tap a pack to use it. A look keeps its pack: choose another look, come back, and it is still in the pack you left it in.

UplinkMist, a pale aqua page with deep teal ink, always light. Lamplight, warm words on a brown-black page for reading late, always dark.
RunestoneResource packs: pixel art that players of the game it is modelled on have made for its windows, buttons and chat parchment. It comes with Duckscape.
Sharp, Dial-UpNone yet.

Runestone's packs are not part of the app. Each is its artist's own work, kept in the packs' hub on GitHub, and the app fetches the one you use to your device: Duckscape by itself, the first time you use the look, and any other when you tap Get a resource pack and choose it. Until a pack has arrived, and with no connection, the look is drawn in plain stone; the pack's row says Fetching, or Try again if it couldn't be fetched.

Tap Plain stone to draw the look with no pack. It stays that way, and nothing is fetched, until you choose a pack again. Tap the pack in use to stop using it or to remove it from the device. Packs are fetched by the app on a phone or a computer; in a browser Runestone is always plain stone.

Make your own look

Tap Make your own look and choose four colours. The preview at the top changes as you go.

BackgroundThe page behind everything.
SurfaceCards, fields and other people's bubbles.
TextThe words.
AccentYour bubbles, Send, and whatever is chosen.

Choose Light or Dark too: your look is always drawn that way, whatever your phone or computer is set to. Give it a name and tap Keep it and use it. The app works out every other shade from your four colours.

It won't keep a look whose words can't be read on its background or surface. Pick a lighter or darker text colour if it says so.

Your looks sit after the app's in the row of looks. Tap one to use it. Tap the one you're using to Edit it, Copy its code to share, or Remove it.

Share a look

A look is shared as a short code that starts GT1, for example GT1ARAUGBwiK-bq8Hqi90R1c2s. Copy it from the look's menu and send it to anyone.

To use one someone sent you, copy the code (or the whole message it's in), then tap Paste a look's code. You'll see the look first, and can change it before you keep it.

A code holds four colours, light or dark, and a name. Nothing else: it can't run anything or change anything but the colours. A code that isn't a look, or whose words couldn't be read, is turned away.

Readable font

Under Text, set Font to Readable to draw everything in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next. It was made so that no two letters look alike (I, l and 1; O and 0; b and d), which many people with dyslexia or low vision find easier to read. It replaces every look's own font, including yours. Code in messages stays in its fixed-width font.

Text size makes the words in messages smaller or larger.
