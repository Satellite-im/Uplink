Computers

Pair a computer with your phone

Your texts on your computer: scan, match the six digits, and keep it connected.

Pairing puts your phone's texts on your Mac or PC: read them, reply, send pictures. Everything between the two is sealed with a key only they share. Your phone does the texting, so it needs to be on, with Uplink able to run.

Pair them

  1. On the computer: Link an Android phone

    Open Uplink on the computer and choose Link an Android phone (or, later, Settings › Your phone). Choose how they'll talk, then a QR code shows.

  2. On the phone: scan it

    Point the phone's camera at the code, or open Settings › Computers › Pair a computer and tap Scan the code. No camera? Paste the code instead.

  3. Check the six digits

    Both screens show six digits. If they're the same, tap They match on the phone, then say so on the computer. If not, tap They don't match: someone may be in between.

  4. Done

    Paired with MacBook Air. Your conversations arrive on the computer. It keeps what it has read, sealed, so it opens quickly next time.

Keep your phone reachable

Android stops apps it thinks are idle. So texts keep reaching the computer overnight, let Uplink run in the background: Settings › Computers › Battery use explains how, and opens the right Android page.

While paired, a quiet Paired with… notification stays on the phone: that's Android's rule for apps that keep running.

Unpair

On the phone, Settings › Computers, tap the computer, then Unpair. It stops showing your texts, and its key is deleted. On the computer, Settings › Your phone has the same, and Clear what is kept removes what it stored.

If it won't connect

  • On the same Wi-Fi, the computer finds the phone by itself. On Windows, or a network that blocks that, it goes over the relay instead (choose Local network, then relay or Relay only).
  • macOS asks once whether Uplink may find devices on your network: say yes.
  • If the phone was asleep a long time, open Uplink on it once.
