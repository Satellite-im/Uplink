Phrases and tokens

How a plugin finds words in a text: phrases, tokens like {digits:6}, and what a match gives it.

A plugin finds words in a text with phrases: plain words, like your bill is ready, with tokens where something varies, like amount due {amount}. There are no regular expressions and no code. The app turns each phrase into a search of its own making, with every repeat in it bounded, so a phrase can't run away with a phone however long or strange the text it reads.

A phrase

A phrase is 1 to 120 characters on one line, keeping the rules for words in a plugin. Read from left to right:

  • { starts a token, which runs to the next }: a name, then optionally :N or :N-M. {digits:6} is {digits:6-6}, exactly six digits.
  • {{ is a plain {, and }} a plain }.
  • A run of spaces is one gap, which matches any run of space in the text: one space, several, a line break.
  • Everything else is matched as it is: letters in any case, and every other character as itself. A . is a full stop, never "any character".

The rules a phrase keeps

A phrase that breaks one of these doesn't load, and says which at its place in the file (rules.tags[0].any[2]). They're checked in this order, and the first one broken is the problem given.

RuleBroken byProblem
Two tokens need something between them.{digits:4}{digits:4}phrase_tokens_touch
A token can't touch a letter or digit. Punctuation may touch one: #{digits:4-10} and code:{code} are fine.bill{digits:4}phrase_token_touches_word
{words} needs a space on each side, so it can't be first or last.{words:1-2} billphrase_words_edge
{url} must be followed by a space or the end of the phrase.{url}.phrase_url_followed
At most 4 tokens, and only one {words} and one {url} among them.a {digits} b {digits} c {digits} d {digits} e {digits}phrase_too_many_tokens
A phrase with no token needs at least two letters or digits.aphrase_too_short

Besides those: a token the app doesn't know, numbers it can't take, a { with no } or a } on its own is phrase_bad_token ({digits:0}, {digits:9-4}, {foo}, code {code:3}, go {words:5}, x {word:2} y, bill }). An empty phrase, one over 120 characters, one with a space at its start or end, a line break or an invisible character is phrase_bad_text.

How a phrase matches

  • Whole words. A phrase that starts or ends with a letter or digit matches only whole words there: bill finds "bill" in "Your bill is ready", but nothing in "Your billing statement".
  • Any case. Letters match in either case, accented ones too: your appt finds "YOUR APPT", and café finds "CAFÉ".
  • Curly quotes and dashes. Before matching, the text and the phrase both have curly quotes made straight, dashes made - and odd spaces made plain ones, so don't forget finds "Don’t forget" written with a curly apostrophe.
  • The first 2,000 characters of a text are read, and the rest isn't.
  • The anchor. A phrase's longest run of plain letters and digits (a to z, 0 to 9), at least three long, is its anchor: the first, where two are as long. The phrase is only tried on a text that has its anchor in it, in any case, which is how hundreds of phrases stay quick. your code is {digits:4-8}'s anchor is your, and tracking no. {code:8-24}'s is tracking. A phrase with no such run, like {phone}, at {time} or café, is anchorless: it's tried on every text, so a plugin may have only 8.

What a match gives

A match gives two things: the whole of what the phrase matched, and what its first token found. A tag rule's detail keeps one of them for its tag, for a row or a copy button to show:

  • {"from": "token"}: what the first token found. amount due: {amount} on "Amount due: 45,00 EUR." keeps 45,00 EUR.
  • {"from": "match"}: the whole match. The same phrase keeps "Amount due: 45,00 EUR".

Each is trimmed, and an empty one keeps nothing: your {words:0-3} has shipped matches "Your has shipped", with no detail.

A slow plugin

A phrase can't run away, but a plugin of 200 phrases on a busy phone could still be slow, so the app times every plugin from the Marketplace or in development as it reads each text:

  • A text that takes it 20 milliseconds or more is read again at once; if that takes as long, it's a strike. So a slow plugin is caught, and a phone that was only busy for a moment isn't held against it.
  • More than 100 milliseconds of reading in any one second is a strike too, where its texts took it a millisecond or more each on average. The app reads every conversation again whenever a switch or a setting changes, and a plugin that's quick on each text isn't held against that.
  • More than half of any one second spent reading is a strike however quick it is on each text, so a plugin that's nearly a millisecond a text can't hold the app for seconds every time a switch changes.
  • The first text after it starts never counts: that's when its phrases are made ready.
  • Five strikes in ten minutes pause it. It does nothing while paused, its page says Paused: it was slowing the app down. with Turn it back on, and the pause is kept, across restarts, until then or a new version.

Where a phrase has a token, "Gives" is what its first token found; where it has none, the whole match.

The person's own words

A words setting lets the person type words of their own, a line each or with commas between. A tag rule's wordsFrom names that setting, and each of the first 50 entries, cut to 60 characters, becomes a phrase that joins the rule's any. Their words are taken as plain words: braces in them are plain braces, never tokens. They match as any phrase does, as whole words in any case, and an entry that can't be a phrase (a single letter, say) is passed over without a word.

Bills' More words for bills is one, with council tax, water rates as the example in its field.
