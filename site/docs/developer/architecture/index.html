The source

How the app is built

The major parts of Uplink, where each lives in the source, and what it talks to.

Uplink is one Flutter app for Android, iPhone, Mac, Windows, Linux and the browser, a small relay server, and a few helper programs. This page is a map of the source for someone about to read or change it: what each part is for, where it lives, and what it talks to. Paths are from the repository's root (github.com/InfamousVague/ghost-messaging).

Textslib/sms.dart
Peoplelib/friends, lib/me, lib/account
Circles, spaces, callslib/circles, lib/voice, lib/files
Other networkslib/inbox

One app, several ways of running

Every screen asks for what it needs through one object, Sms.instance (lib/sms.dart): the conversations, a thread's messages, sending, the settings. Sms passes each call by name to whatever is set as Sms.calls, and which object that is decides how the app runs. The screens are the same in every case.

Answered byWhereUsed when
The platform channel ghost_messaging/smslib/sms.dart, android/…/MainActivity.ktAn Android phone. Kotlin reads and writes Android's own SMS and MMS database.
OverlayCallslib/standalone/overlay.dartAndroid, wrapped round the channel: conversations with @username friends are answered in Dart, the rest passed on.
StandaloneCallslib/standalone/standalone.dartAn iPhone, a computer with no phone linked, or a browser: the app's own network and nothing from a phone. What it keeps is behind StandaloneStore (lib/standalone/store.dart): a folder and the Keychain (StandaloneFiles), or in a browser sealed records in IndexedDB (WebStandaloneStore, lib/web/store.dart).
LinkCallslib/desktop/remote.dartA computer paired with an Android phone: each call becomes a sealed request to the phone.
DeskOwnCallslib/desktop/own_calls.dartA paired computer the phone has handed its identity to: friends and circles run on the computer itself, texts still come from the phone.

lib/main.dart chooses: a computer goes to desktopMain() (lib/desktop/main.dart), an iPhone starts on its own, and Android starts on the channel. A browser's build has its own entry, lib/web/main.dart, which goes to lib/web/app.dart. From there both paths load the person's choices (AppState, lib/app_state.dart), the plugins, the API, and then the network through startPeople (lib/people.dart), which starts the routes, friends, what checks the relay, groups, spaces and circles in that order.

The app keeps no copy of your texts: they live in Android's SMS and MMS database, which is why changing texting apps loses nothing. The Kotlin side is in android/app/src/main/kotlin/com/infamousvague/ghostmessaging.

Routes: how apps reach each other

Everything between two Uplink apps (a friend's message, a call's setup, a space's chat) goes through lib/net. Its idea is the route: one way of getting bytes to another install, behind an interface (Carrier, lib/net/carrier.dart) so a new one can be added without the app above knowing.

PartWhereWhat it does
Envelope and packetlib/net/packet.dartAn envelope is a small JSON message with a kind (text, call, room, hello…). A packet is an envelope as a route carries it: a byte of version and flags, the sender's 16-byte name, then the envelope, sealed. The flags say whether it is sealed, a part, sealed for forward secrecy, or passed on unopened by another device of the account. Long ones cross a short route in parts.
The switchboardlib/net/switchboard.dartChooses a route for each thing sent, falls back to the next, seals and opens, joins parts, drops second copies, and answers what asks for an answer.
The hub routelib/net/hub_carrier.dart, hub_link.dartA connection to the relay server's hub. Also where presence and rooms come from. Its protocol. What carries the connection is behind HubLink: dart:io's WebSocket on a phone or a computer (hub_link_io.dart), the browser's own in a page (hub_link_web.dart).
The direct routelib/net/p2p, native/p2pPackets straight between devices, with no server between. Below.
The SMS routelib/net/sms_carrier.dart, android/…/NetSms.ktPackets as data texts: 133 bytes each, six at most, costing a text each, and able to wake a closed app.
Keys and sealinglib/net/crypto.dart, fast_gcm.dart, android/…/Net.ktP-256 key agreement, HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM. Kotlin and Dart are held to the same bytes by tests on both sides. fast_gcm.dart has two halves that give the same bytes: one for 64-bit integers, one for a browser's.
Forward secrecylib/net/forward_secrecy.dartPrekeys each device makes and deletes, and the sealing under a one-off key for each packet. Below.
Where people livelib/net/home_record.dart, homes.dart, relay_ask.dart, relay_choice.dartThe signed record of which relay an account uses, looking a username up on another relay, and moving to a relay of one's own. Below.
Checking the relaylib/net/ledger.dart, name_watch.dartWhat the relay says, compared with what friends' devices say. Below.
The app's hold on it alllib/net/net.dartNet: starts the routes, knows who can be reached, and is what the rest of the app sends through.

The routes are tried in the person's order (Settings › Routes, drawn by lib/ui/network.dart); where they have chosen none it is the hub, the direct route, the local network, then SMS (Switchboard.order). One is passed over when it is off, down, can't reach that person, can't hold the packet, or costs money and the message isn't one allowed to. Three routes are written: the hub, the direct route and SMS. The order names a local-network route (lan) that has no carrier yet.

Forward secrecy

A packet to a friend used to be sealed with the pair's key alone, which comes from the two accounts' long-term keys. Whoever later learned an account's words and had kept the packets could open them all. ForwardSecrecy (lib/net/forward_secrecy.dart) closes that:

  • Each device makes a key pair of its own, a prekey, that is never in the account's backup. It makes a new one every day and deletes each one's private half after fifteen days.
  • A device offers its newest prekey inside the sealed envelopes it sends (Envelope.fs), so no server is needed and a relay that knows nothing of it carries it.
  • A packet to someone whose devices have offered prekeys is sealed under a key pair made for that packet alone and forgotten after. The pair's key still goes into the sealing, which is what says who sent it.
  • To a build from before, or over a route that carries only small packets (a data text), the switchboard seals the old way. A device that can't open a forward-secret packet answers with its prekey (envelope kind fsk) so the next try reaches it.

A circle's key is changed when someone is put out of it (lib/circles/circle_keys.dart). docs/FORWARD_SECRECY.md in the repository is the whole design.

The direct route

The direct route (P2pCarrier, id p2p, shown as Direct) carries the same sealed packets between devices themselves. The relay still comes first: it is the fast path and the one that keeps things for someone who is away. Direct is a way through when the relay can't carry something, and what the relay is checked over.

PartWhereWhat it does
The corenative/p2p (Rust, on iroh)A node: a key made on the device, whose public half is its node id. It dials, gets through NATs, falls back to a link helper (iroh's relay server, which is not Uplink's relay), and turns away any node the app has not allowed. It publishes nothing and looks nobody up.
The contractlib/net/p2p/node.dartP2pNode: what the Dart above relies on. The real one is IrohNode over dart:ffi (node_ffi.dart, node_ffi_io.dart), made by makeP2pNode() (node_factory.dart); tests use a stand-in mesh (test/support/fake_p2p.dart); a browser has none. On Android P2pCore.kt loads the library when the first node starts, and not before.
Device cardslib/net/p2p/card.dartDeviceCard: an account's signed word that a node id is one of its devices. Anyone can check one with nothing but the card.
Who links, and whenlib/net/p2p/p2p.dartP2p: links only with the account's own devices and with friends. Cards and whereabouts are told in sealed envelopes of kind p2p (card, card?, meet). P2pPolicy says when a node runs: a computer's stays up, a phone's is started for something to say and stopped two minutes after its last stream closes.
The carryinglib/net/p2p/p2p_carrier.dartSends a packet to every device a card is held for, on a stream that is open or dialled by where the device last said it was. Each end of a stream proves whose device it is before anything is taken. It never keeps anything.

Link helpers are named by the relay as an app signs in to its hub (HubCarrier.helpers, from the relay's GHOST_LINK_HELPERS). With none, devices link only where they can reach each other's addresses as they are.

Not switched on yetThe route, its settings and its tests are in the source, and Net starts it wherever Net.p2pNode gives it a node. Nothing in lib/ sets that yet: only tests do. Until a build wires makeP2pNode() in, Net.p2p is null and Settings › Routes shows no Direct.

Checking the relay

The relay is trusted with little, and two parts check what is left.

  • The name watch (NameWatch, lib/net/name_watch.dart). A friend added by username was found by asking the relay who holds the name. The app asks the same again about once a day for each such friend, and of its own name as it signs in. An answer that is a different key is shown on that friend, and the friend is told. An add-me link (lib/friends/add_link.dart) carries the start of the key after #, and Buddies.add refuses an answer that doesn't match. The lookups share one budget (lib/me/lookup_budget.dart).
  • The ledger (Ledger, lib/net/ledger.dart). The switchboard tells it what was sent to each friend, by which route and what the route said, and what came (SendLog). It keeps names and times for seven days, never a message's words, in a folder no backup takes (lib/net/kept_files.dart). When a direct stream to a friend's device opens, the two compare (envelope kind ldg): what never arrived is sent again straight, and what the relay said of it is put down. It compares only over direct links, so it runs where the direct route does.

Settings › Routes shows what both found (relayCheckSection, lib/ui/network.dart).

Friends on another relay

An app talks to one relay. Anyone can run their own, and relays can pass sealed packets to each other, so two friends need not share one.

  • A home record (HomeRecord, lib/net/home_record.dart; the relay's twin is relay/lib/home_record.dart) is an account's signed statement of the relay addresses it lives at, with a count that only goes up. Both sides are held to test/home_record_vectors.json.
  • Homes (lib/net/homes.dart) makes this account's record, gives it to the relay as the app signs in, and keeps friends' records. Friends tell each other theirs in a sealed envelope of kind home (lib/friends/buddies.dart).
  • @maya@relay.example names someone on another relay. Homes.whois asks the app's own relay, which asks the other over a link. Where its own relay doesn't link with others, the app asks the other relay itself, under a key made for that one question (whoisAt, lib/net/relay_ask.dart).
  • lib/net/relay_choice.dart is moving to another relay: what a typed address becomes, and whether an Uplink relay answers there before the app moves.

Every packet still goes through the app's own relay. The passing on is the relay's work. Usernames, account backups, circles, spaces and calls stay on the relay they were made on.

Who someone is

  • An install is a P-256 key pair. Its name everywhere on the network is the first 16 bytes of the SHA-256 of its public key, in hex. The relay knows installs by that name and nothing else. Every device of one account holds the same key, so each also has a device name of its own (hub_device, made in Net.start), which the hub, forward secrecy and circles' keys tell devices apart by.
  • An account (lib/account) is twelve recovery words. From them come an address that names the account, and the keys that seal its backup, which is kept on the relay sealed twice: once under the password, once under the words. How your account works is the plain account of it. In a browser the password is stretched by web/argon2id.js on a worker (lib/account/argon2_browser.dart).
  • A profile (lib/me/me.dart): the username, linked handles and verified number, kept by the relay's directory as hashes. What dresses a profile (a border, a banner, a name effect) is lib/me/cosmetics.dart.
  • Friends: by number (lib/friends/friends.dart, for people encryption is on with) and by username (lib/friends/buddies.dart). Groups of friends are lib/friends/groups.dart: each message goes to each member by the routes.
  • Where an account lives is its home record, and which nodes are its devices its device cards. Both are signed by the account's key.

Circles, spaces and calls

A space (in the code, a hangout or voice channel) is 32 random bytes, its seed. The room's name on the hub, the token that lets someone in and the key everything in it is sealed with are all made from the seed (VoiceChannel, lib/voice/voice.dart), so whoever holds a space's link holds all three and the relay holds none.

  • lib/voice/channels.dart (Voice): the spaces someone is in, and making, sharing, joining and leaving them.
  • lib/voice/hangout.dart (HangoutRoom): a space's chat. History is passed between members; the hub only holds packets for whoever is away.
  • lib/voice/rtc.dart: the call itself, WebRTC from each member to each other. Offers, answers and candidates are sealed with the space's key and said in its room; sound and picture never go through the relay's hub.
  • A way through when two devices can't reach each other. A connection looks for a path with public STUN servers, and with a TURN server where the relay has one: the hub hands whoever joins a room a short-lived username and password for it (relay/lib/turn.dart, HubCarrier.callRelay, RtcMedia.iceServers). Where one was handed out, a connection starts through it alone and falls back to trying every path (FallbackMedia, lib/voice/voice.dart). The TURN server passes WebRTC's own encrypted packets and can't hear the call.
  • lib/voice/open_space.dart: an open space, a call with a floor on top. Places and groups are said in the call's room, sealed like its offers, and how far apart two people stand becomes a gain on each one's sound (PlacedMedia); the floor is drawn by lib/ui/open_space_view.dart. Followed step by step in How the parts work together.
  • lib/voice/friend_calls.dart (FriendCalls): ringing one friend, with no space made. The call's seed is made from the two friends' names, so only they can work it out; ringing, declining and cancelling are sealed voice envelopes sent by the routes. On Android the ringing is IncomingCall.kt.
  • In a call: the auto leveler (lib/voice/leveler.dart), the soundboard (lib/voice/soundboard.dart: only a sound's id is said in the room, each device plays its own copy), the live voice changer (lib/voice/live_changer.dart, an effect in native/voicefx), and on a computer screen sharing (lib/ui/screen_share.dart). Each of the first three is switched by a plugin of the same name in lib/plugins.
  • Spotify in a circle's call (lib/voice/spotify.dart): the queue is held by the DJ's device (spotify_queue.dart, spotify_deck.dart) and said in the call's room as mu; the music goes to each listener over a connection of its own (spotify_link.dart). On a Mac (macos/Runner/Spotify.swift, SpotifySound.swift, MusicLink.swift) it is AppleScript, a Core Audio tap on Spotify's processes, and a second WebRTC factory whose sound device is that sound; on Windows (windows/runner/spotify.cpp, spotify_sound.cpp) it is Windows' media controls, WASAPI's loopback of Spotify's process tree, and a track of flutter_webrtc's own. tool/spotify/check.sh listens to the Mac's link without a sound.
  • Recordings: lib/voice/recorder.dart writes one MP4 with a track for each person, recording_playback.dart opens one again in the mixer, and on Windows mp4_merge.dart joins the one-track files windows/runner/call_recorder.cpp makes.
  • lib/voice/roles.dart: a space's roles, as a numbered list signed by its owner.
  • lib/circles: a circle is one seed and a signed manifest listing its spaces, roles and settings (CircleManifest, lib/circles/circle.dart). Members pass the newest manifest to each other. When someone is put out of a circle its key is changed, so the seed they keep opens nothing said after (CircleKeys, lib/circles/circle_keys.dart).
  • lib/files: a Files space. Files are sealed on the device in 256 KiB pieces (lib/net/file_seal.dart) and kept by the relay, which sees sizes and never names or contents. My files (lib/files/drive.dart) is one more Files space to the relay: its seed is made from the key the account's devices share (Me.mineKey), and every device writes to it with one key pair made from that seed, so each is the drive's owner and none has to be told of it.

Other networks

lib/inbox puts other services' chats in the same list. Each is an InboxNetwork (lib/inbox/inbox.dart) with its own way of connecting:

NetworkHow
TelegramTDLib, loaded into the app through dart:ffi (lib/inbox/td_json.dart, td_json_io.dart).
Signal, WhatsApp, MatrixA helper program each (sidecar/), started by the app and spoken to in lines of JSON over its input and output. Each helper's PROTOCOL.md says what is said. Their chats are written to disk off the drawing thread by KeptWriter (lib/inbox/kept_writer.dart).
Messenger, Instagram, Discord, X, SlackAn account helper each (sidecar/messenger, instagram, discord, twitter, slack), all speaking one protocol (sidecar/ACCOUNT_HELPERS.md), so one class drives them all: AccountHelper (lib/inbox/account_helper.dart), told apart by a HelperService. None of these services allows other apps. A helper signs in as the person, with the cookies of a browser signed in to their account, and the app says so first.
Google Chat, Microsoft TeamsTheir own web APIs, straight from the app.
Bluesky, MastodonWritten (bluesky.dart, mastodon.dart), listed as coming soon and never started.

The cookies an account helper signs in with come one of two ways. Pasted: a request copied from the browser's developer tools, read by lib/inbox/cookie_paste.dart. Or synced: the browser extension (extension/) reads them, seals them with a key only the account's devices have, and keeps them on the relay; each device reads them back and signs in (CurlSync, lib/inbox/curl_sync.dart).

lib/inbox/human_check.dart holds a first message from someone new on a chosen network until its sender answers a small question. Several networks are also cards in the Marketplace, sharing one switch with Settings (lib/plugins/networks.dart).

No chat from these networks goes through Uplink's relay. The sealed cookies are the one thing of theirs it keeps, and it can't read them. A browser has no helper programs, so the web client has none of these networks.

Paired computers

A computer paired with a phone shows the phone's texts by asking the phone for them. The computer's half is lib/desktop/link (PhoneLink, LinkSession); the phone's is DeskSession.kt. They connect over the local network where they can and through the relay's pairing channel where they can't, and either way every frame is sealed with keys made from the pairing, so the relay passes bytes it can't read. What the computer has read is kept sealed on its disk (lib/desktop/cache.dart).

Plugins and the Marketplace

A plugin adds to the conversations list and to texts through a few fixed points (lib/plugins/plugin.dart): tagging a message, a section or tab in the list, a button, a rule for what arrives. The built-in ones are Dart classes (lib/plugins/registry.dart). One from another developer is data, never code: a JSON file (lib/plugins/pack) the app reads into those same points, held to what it declared by GatedHost.

Which plugins are on is synced between an account's devices (PluginSync, lib/plugins/plugin_sync.dart): a list in the same sealed slot on the relay as the extension's cookies. A circle can hold some plugins for all its members: the soundboard and the auto leveler are on in a circle's spaces when the circle has them on.

The Marketplace (lib/market, and relay/lib/market.dart on the server) lists reviewed plugins. An approval is a reviewer's signature over the plugin's id, version and its file's SHA-256, checked against keys the app ships with, at install and at every start: the server can withhold a plugin but can't make one look reviewed.

The relay server

relay/bin/relay.dart reads its options and environment, makes each part over one data folder, and starts one HTTP server; relay/lib/relay.dart sends each request to its part.

What it can see is the same throughout: which installs talk, when, and how much. Linked to other relays, it also sees which of its people talk to people on which relay. Its HTTP routes and its WebSocket protocols are documented in full, and docs/SELF_HOSTING.md in the repository says how to run one and what a relay of your own reaches.

A relay in the appThe relay is plain Dart so that the app can one day run the same hub in itself, and the seams for it are in place (HubLink, HubSocket, the ghost-relay:// address a home record can hold). The hosting itself is not in the source yet.

The app in a browser

The web client is the same Dart, built by dart2js from its own entry (lib/web/main.dart), running as one more device of an account. It is the standalone way of running: lib/web/app.dart sets StandaloneCalls over the browser's store and calls startPeople, as a computer with no phone linked does.

PartWhereWhat it does
The startlib/web/app.dartOpens the store, works out the hub's address (the one the build names, or /hub beside a page served at /app/), and starts only what a page can run.

Code that can't compile or run in a browser is split, and the whole of lib/ goes through the split:

  • lib/platform_info.dart answers "is this Android?" in place of dart:io's Platform, which throws in a browser.
  • lib/aside.dart (runAside) is Isolate.run where there are isolates, and the same work in place where there are none.
  • Files that need dart:ffi or dart:io have two halves chosen by a conditional import: td_json, lib/files/apple_sha, lib/account/gzip, lib/net/hub_link, fast_gcm, kept_files, lib/net/p2p/node_ffi.

Guard tests read the source to hold it to this (test/platform_info_test.dart, test/aside_test.dart, test/source_guards_test.dart), and the Android and Mac releases run them before they build.

Not in a browser, as its own About says (notInBrowserYet, lib/web/settings.dart): calls, voice messages and sounds, Files spaces, plugins and the Marketplace, chats on other networks, and texts through a phone. It has no direct route either, so it keeps no ledger.

Screens

  • lib/ui/shell.dart (AppShell) is the frame: the list and what is open beside it, side by side where the window is wide.
  • lib/ui/conversations.dart is the list, lib/ui/thread.dart a conversation, lib/ui/hangout_screen.dart a space, lib/ui/voice_screen.dart a call.
  • lib/ui/settings.dart and settings_panes.dart are Settings, built from the rows in lib/kit. lib/ui/network.dart is Settings › Routes: each route, the relay in use, Direct, and what checking the relay found.
  • lib/desktop/ui is what only a computer has: the set-up wizard, the title bar, the update bar. A computer's own voice messages are lib/desktop/desk_voice.dart and desk_player.dart.
  • lib/art holds the icons, lib/looks.dart the looks, lib/custom_theme.dart a look of the person's own, and lib/effects.dart, stickers.dart and reactions.dart what texts can wear.
  • What a message's words can do: formatting markers drawn as bold, code, quotes and spoilers (lib/message_format.dart), and emoji by shortcode as they are typed (lib/emoji_shortcodes.dart).
  • What a conversation can do: be snoozed until a time (lib/snooze.dart), be exported as a Markdown file (lib/export_chat.dart), and have a person or a message in it reported (lib/safety/abuse_report.dart, lib/ui/report_sheet.dart).

The app's own API

lib/api is a registry of methods (conversations.list, messages.send…), each with its parameters, what it answers and an example. The same methods are called from Settings' own test area, over HTTP on the device, and from anywhere through the relay, sealed. See API overview and Postman.
