Submit it to the Marketplace

The submission form, what review looks at, listing it, and sending a new version.

When your plugin works, send it to the Marketplace from Marketplace › Submit a plugin. A person at Uplink reviews it, and once it's approved anyone can add it, under your username. You can send new versions, take it off the Marketplace and list it again from Marketplace › My submissions.

What you need first

  • A username, claimed in Settings › Me a week ago or more: your plugin is listed under it ("A username can submit plugins a week after it's claimed.").
  • Your Uplink identity on the device you send from. A computer that follows its phone doesn't have one of its own: on it, use Settings › Me › Become the same you as your phone, or send from the phone.
  • The app on Uplink's own server. Plugins are reviewed there; if Settings › Routes has the app on another, Send for review says so and waits until you switch back.
  • Not an iPhone: plugins from other developers aren't on iPhone, so neither is Submit a plugin.

Developer mode isn't needed to submit, though you'll want it to try your plugin first. The Marketplace may also ask for a verified number (Settings › Me › Number).

From your computer to your phone

You'll likely write the file on a computer and send it from your phone. Any of these gets it there:

  • adb push yours.ghostplugin /sdcard/Download/, then in the form tap Choose the plugin file and pick it from Downloads.
  • Load it in Developer mode from a link through adb reverse (how), then tap Use one in development in the form.
  • Load it with plugins.devInstall from your computer (how), then Use one in development.
  • Or send it from the computer itself: there, Settings › Me › Become the same you as your phone, then Paste the plugin, or on a Mac Choose the plugin file.

  • Send for review is ready once there are no problems and the switch is on. It says Sent for review, and opens My submissions with the new one marked.

Listing it

  • List it once it's approved, on by default, lists it as soon as it's approved. Off, it's approved and waits; list it from Marketplace › My submissions when you're ready.
  • Take it off the Marketplace, on its heading in My submissions: "People who added it keep it. Nobody new can add it until you list it again."
  • List it again puts it back, where you were the one who took it off. Where review took it off, only review can list it again.

What review looks at

The reviewer reads the whole plugin in the same app you have, with these in front of them:

  • What it can do: the same lines people will see before they add it, made from the file, and for a new version, what's new in them.
  • Look closely: every rule that acts on texts, gathered so none hides among the phrases: every number it names, every incoming rule and the switch it needs, every reply it writes and every copy it makes, its link, every phrase with no anchor, and what changed among these since the last version.
  • Checks: that the file is the very one the Marketplace holds, sent the way the app sends it, and every problem and warning.
  • Try it, on the samples and on texts of the reviewer's own.
  • The file, all of it, and what changed since your last version or since changes were asked for.
  • Your listing: the screenshots (with no real person's texts or number), what's new, your help link and your notes; and your username's history.

Then it's Approve and list, Ask for changes or Reject, the last two always with a note saying why.

Where it stands

Marketplace › My submissions lists your plugins, each with whether it's listed, and every version you've sent under it:

You don't have to keep looking: when one changes the app tells you, once: "My bills 1.0.0: changes asked for. See Marketplace › My submissions.", "My bills 1.0.0 was approved and is listed." (or "… was approved. List it from My submissions when you're ready."), "My bills 1.0.0 wasn't accepted.", or "My bills was taken off the Marketplace."

Changes asked for, and new versions

  • A submission's page has its notes, the reviewer's and yours, each with the date; what it can do; its screenshots; and Copy the package, the file as sent.
  • Submit a new version opens the form with that file and your listing's words, its screenshots kept, and its version one higher where it was approved (the same where changes were asked for). Load your corrected file, and the form keeps what you'd written in it.
  • Sent again after changes, the reviewer sees what changed since they asked, and your note.
  • Once a new version of a listed plugin is approved, everyone who has it is offered it as an update, and its screenshots carry over unless you removed them.

Withdrawing one

  • While a submission is waiting, in review or has changes asked for, Withdraw on its page takes it back.
  • Once a version is approved, take the plugin off the Marketplace instead (above): people who have it keep it.
  • Review can withdraw a version it approved, if it turns out to break the guidelines. It then switches off on every device that has it, and My submissions says Withdrawn by review with the reason.

What "Reviewed" means

Approving a plugin is a signature, made on the reviewer's own device, over exactly that file: its id, its version and its SHA-256. Every Uplink app carries the reviewer's key and checks that signature when the plugin is added and every time the app starts, so no server, ours included, can pass off a changed file, or one nobody reviewed, as reviewed. A change of one character is a new file that needs a new review.

Reviewed on a card, and Reviewed by Uplink on a plugin's page, say that a person read that file and its listing, and found it does what it says within the guidelines. It says nothing about who you are.
